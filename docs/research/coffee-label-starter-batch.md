# Coffee beverage starter batch — manufacturer label research

**Research date:** September 26, 2026  
**Scope:** 21 U.S.-market, packaged ready-to-drink (RTD) coffee/cold-brew or coffee concentrate variants drawn only from the manufacturers' public product pages. This is a starter queue, not a claim that it represents every coffee sold in the U.S.

## Provenance and import rule

Every item below has a first-party page which publicly displays an ingredient statement and Nutrition Facts, except the single row explicitly marked `Partial`. That qualifies it as a **manufacturer-label-source record**, not an in-hand **package-verified** record: none of these pages provided a matched UPC/GTIN and photographed package version for this research pass. Store the URL, retrieval date, raw label text, and package/serving evidence; do not overwrite a later label version.

For coffee concentrates, distinguish the **concentrate serving** from the beverage prepared as directed. The Starbucks pages describe a 6 fl oz concentrate serving that makes 12 fl oz; their pages do not state the bottle's net contents, so that field is intentionally `unverified`. STōK reports roughly 120–170 mg caffeine per serving for its ordinary cold brews as a family-level range, not as exact per-variant values; its decaf FAQ says about 97% of bean caffeine is removed. Do not attach either figure as an exact package-level caffeine value. [STōK FAQ](https://www.stokbrew.com/faq/)

## Implementation notes

1. Import these as `manufacturer_label_source` / `catalog_label_match`, **not** as `package_verified`. Capture the exact net quantity and UPC only when a matching package image, GS1/manufacturer feed, or reliable FDC record confirms them.
2. Keep the ingredient statement verbatim and use the short descriptions above only as a research index. Ingredients are label-order facts; they do not disclose individual amounts, contaminants, or a complete formulation.
3. Add a `preparation_basis` field for concentrates (`as_sold_concentrate`, `prepared_1_to_1`) so that the interface never compares a concentrate's 6 fl oz nutrition directly to a 9 or 12 fl oz RTD drink.
4. The factual signals ready for the existing policy layer are: declared added sugars, declared caffeine where the manufacturer provides an exact value, milk/oat base, and named sweeteners/flavor declarations. They do **not** establish that a drink is broadly healthy, unhealthy, harmful, safe in unlimited quantities, or medically suitable.

## Gaps before a production expansion

- This batch intentionally has strong coverage of cold brew/concentrates and La Colombe canned lattes, but does not yet verify bottled Starbucks Frappuccino, bottled Dunkin' iced coffee, STōK seasonal creamers, or independent RTD brands such as Califia, Chameleon, or Stumptown. Add those only after locating a first-party label page or a permissible package image/UPC source.
- Recheck sources and package versions on every refresh. Dunkin' explicitly asks consumers to use the package for the most current information, which reinforces treating web data as a dated source snapshot rather than permanent label truth.
