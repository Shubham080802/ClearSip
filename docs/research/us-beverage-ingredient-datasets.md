# Recognized datasets for US packaged beverages and ingredients

**Research date:** September 25, 2026  
**Decision:** Use **USDA FoodData Central (FDC) — Branded Foods** as ClearSip's primary, legally reusable catalog source for US packaged beverages. It is the strongest public source for product-level ingredient statements, barcodes, brand identity, package/serving data, and label-declared nutrients. Supplement it with manufacturer/package verification and a separately licensed commercial feed only if completeness and freshness requirements justify the cost.

There is no public, authoritative census of *every* drink sold in the United States. Products are introduced, reformulated, discontinued, regionally distributed, and sometimes voluntarily submitted. Therefore ClearSip should say “catalog match” or “package verified,” never “all US beverages” or “complete ingredients,” unless the exact product, package, market, and label version have evidence.

## Why FDC Branded Foods is the recommended dataset

FDC's own [field dictionary](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/portal-data/external/dataDictionary) defines `ingredients` as “the list of ingredients (as it appears on the product label).” The same branded-food record includes `gtin_upc`, `brand_owner`, serving size/unit, category, data source, modified/available/discontinued dates, and market country. USDA's branded-food documentation says the public-private partnership receives label information from industry providers, offers historical product update logs including ingredient-list edits, and makes current records available through the API.

For initial scale, use the [bulk Branded Foods release](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/) in an offline ETL job, rather than loading a multi-gigabyte file in a Vercel request. The official downloads page currently lists CSV and JSON Branded Foods releases. For app traffic and deltas, call FDC search/detail from the server and cache records with fetch time, FDC ID, GTIN, publication date, raw ingredient statement, and payload hash.

FDC is authoritative about the source and record it publishes; it is **not** a laboratory analysis and does not make every beverage record complete. USDA says participation is voluntary, industry data providers are responsible for product descriptions/nutrients/serving size/ingredients, and a missing nutrient does not mean a zero value. Its data-quality material also explains that reformulations and certain package changes can require a new GTIN. Treat a product as `product + formulation/label version + package/GTIN + market`, rather than a single timeless brand row.

## Implementable source policy

1. Import FDC Branded Foods where `market_country = United States`, filter to beverage categories/keywords, and retain the raw *label-order* ingredient statement. Do not pretend that an ingredient list reveals undisclosed amounts, proprietary flavour components, contaminants, or every processing aid.
2. Store FDC as `source_type = usda_fdc_branded`, `verification = catalog_match`. Promote a record to `package_verified` only after an in-hand label image or a permitted manufacturer feed matches its UPC, net contents, and ingredient statement.
3. Normalize individual ingredient tokens for search/classification, but keep the verbatim statement and source snapshot. FDA explains that food labels generally list ingredients in descending order by weight and that some are lawfully grouped as flavours/spices or certain colour declarations; a parsed list is therefore a convenience, not a complete recipe. See [FDA's ingredient explanation](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-and-gras-ingredients-information-consumers/types-food-ingredients).
4. Keep FDC and any Open Food Facts/commercial records in separate provenance tables. Dedupe by barcode only after package/market match; never overwrite a prior label version. Schedule monthly FDC deltas and review an item when user OCR conflicts with the catalog.
5. Keep health communication evidence-based and conditional. An ingredient's presence and an FDA regulatory listing do not establish that a specific product is harmful, beneficial, or disease-causing. The app should distinguish label facts from general education and leave unknown quantities as unknown.

## Bottom line

Start the nationwide beverage dataset with **FDC Branded Foods**. It is official USDA-hosted, recognized, broad, ingredient-bearing, accessible through an API/download, and CC0. Use **FDA inventories** for context about individual substances, **NHANES/FNDDS** to decide which beverage categories to prioritize, **Open Food Facts** only with deliberate ODbL compliance, and **NIQ/GDSN** only under a negotiated production licence when the project needs higher commercial completeness or identity matching.
